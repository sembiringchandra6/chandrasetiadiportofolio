Technical Support Maintenance (AUG 2026 - Present)

During my internship as a Technical Support Maintenance intern at PT Sumi Asih, I was directly involved in the maintenance and repair of industrial equipment under the guidance of technicians. In terms of preventive maintenance, I performed upkeep on industrial pumps and conducted routine equipment inspections to ensure the machinery remained reliable and to prevent breakdowns. Regarding corrective maintenance, I carried out the replacement of pump components such as bearings, mechanical seals, and couplings following the technicians' instructions, which provided me with an understanding of the entire repair process, from identifying faults to reassembly. I also assisted with compressor repairs, which included installing components, assembling pistons, and verifying the condition of the machinery following the repairs. I also gained experience in repairing a shaping machine that had sustained internal damage specifically, a broken pin. Since the damage was not visible from the outside, my team and I began by removing the upper section (the head) of the machine to directly identify the broken component. We also inspected the surrounding parts—including the shaft, connecting rod, and internal gears to ensure there was no secondary damage, such as wear or other irregularities that could lead to recurring failures. Throughout this process, I participated in the disassembly, inspection, and reassembly of the machine alongside my team members. This experience helped me develop skills in troubleshooting, teamwork, and communication, as well as the application of safe operating procedures when dismantling machine tools. During my internship, I was assigned a mini-project involving the reverse engineering of a valve cap. The process began with measuring the original component using a caliper, followed by redesigning it in Autodesk Inventor to match the original dimensions. Once the design was finalized, I manufactured the component on a lathe using stainless steel. This project integrated skills in measurement, 3D modeling, technical drawing interpretation, and machining, while also requiring a high level of precision to ensure the final product met the specifications of the original component.

CAD/CAM CNC MACHINING DESIGN and SIMULATION

Manufacturing / CNC Programmer

This project was executed as part of the Final Semester Examination (UAS) for the Machine Tools Practice course, focusing on the seamless integration of CAD (Computer-Aided Design) and CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacturing) workflows within Autodesk Inventor. The entire workflow was performed independently, beginning with the 3D CAD modeling of a solid block plaque featuring a customized embossed text relief representing the student's name and identification number "CHAN 1526423034". Following the design phase, the process transitioned into the CAM configuration stage, where the machining setup was established, the Work Coordinate System (WCS) was defined along the X, Y, and Z axes, and a high-efficiency 2D Adaptive Clearing toolpath strategy was applied using an End Mill cutter. This dynamic roughing strategy was specifically selected to rapidly clear surrounding material while maintaining a constant tool load, thereby reducing cycle times and preventing premature tool wear. The workflow concluded with a comprehensive visual toolpath simulation to verify all machining movements and detect any potential tool or spindle collisions before G-code generation. Although the physically machined part was directly submitted for academic evaluation, the virtual model and toolpaths were fully verified to be 100% precise and production-ready.

ARDUINO BASED SMART CONVEYOR SYSTEM

Production Line Automation: Development of a Smart Conveyor System Prototype

Executed within a 4-member project team for the Production Machinery Manufacturing course, this engineering initiative focused on designing and manufacturing an automated conveyor system engineered to streamline real-time object logging workflows. The project lifecycle initiated with my role as the lead mechanical designer, where I took full responsibility for the 3D CAD modeling using Autodesk Inventor, analyzing material capacities, and establishing critical tolerances for motor and sensor placements. During the physical fabrication phase, I actively contributed to hands-on structural development, performing SMAW (Shielded Metal Arc Welding) operations for the main frame and precision drilling for frame fasteners. In the final automation phase, I collaborated closely with a team member to execute complex wiring and develop control algorithms within the Arduino IDE. The finalized system successfully leveraged an Arduino ESP32 microcontroller interfaced with an ultrasonic sensor for object detection, alongside a DC motor regulated by an L298N driver for stable belt movement, demonstrating my strong capability in unifying mechanical fabrication with advanced industrial automation.
